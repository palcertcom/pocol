Mining as a measurable contribution

PoCol coordinates where miners search and records evidence of their contributions. Understanding the distinction between a miner, a share and a settled reward makes the collaboration model easier to follow.

Who receives an assignment?

Assignments use the eligible miner set derived from the parent state. The design includes registration bonds, activity windows, exit and inactivity-removal rules. These are protocol mechanisms, not evidence that participation is already open on a production network. The current devnet builder sets R_max to zero: its miners are established in genesis, and new registration or reactivation is disabled in that profile.

A share is evidence, not a second block

A share satisfies a target easier than the winning target. The winner commits the included share list in the block, and the winning nonce is also counted as one implicit share. One miner can contribute several shares, so the number of shares is not the number of distinct participants. Shares sample successful hash trials; they do not prove exhaustive search or guarantee that every contribution reaches the winning block.

How rewards are distributed

For a non-final block, the reward pot combines its configured subsidy and transaction priority tips. Base fees are burned separately. The protocol reserves portions for the winner and template proposer, then calculates contribution payments from committed share counts. In a fallback block, the proposer portion goes to the winner. The pot is not simply split equally among connected miners.

Settlement is deferred. The next block records the winner and share owners, and a later system-contract phase applies the payout formula. Its denominator has a configured floor; rounding and defined penalty or payment conditions can burn part of the pot. An eligible identity, an included share and an actual payment are therefore different states. Reorganizations also restore or replace the relevant pending settlement state.

Energy: a trade-off to measure

Dividing the search space does not by itself reduce energy consumption. The research studied policies that idle miners or place them in lower-power states, showing savings alongside changes in accepted-block output and round duration. Those results belong to specific simulations, not benchmarks of pocold. These results do not establish a universal energy-saving percentage, guaranteed mining income or equivalent security.

Any future efficiency comparison should report its workload, hardware, accepted blocks, latency and reference baseline together, so that lower consumption can be assessed alongside the service delivered.
